## Steps
1. Stop the burning: move away from the heat source. If clothes are on fire: stop, drop and roll, or smother with a heavy blanket.
2. Cool the burn under cool (not ice-cold) running water for at least 20 minutes. Start as soon as possible; cooling helps for up to 3 hours after the burn.
3. Remove rings, watches and tight clothing near the burn before swelling starts, unless stuck to the skin.
4. After cooling, cover loosely with cling film laid on in strips, or a clean non-fluffy dressing or plastic bag.
5. For chemical burns, brush off dry powder, then rinse with large amounts of running water for at least 20 minutes, removing contaminated clothing.
6. Keep the person warm; cooling large burns can cause hypothermia, especially in children.
7. Give simple pain relief if available and appropriate.

## Warnings
- Do not use ice, iced water, butter, toothpaste, oil or home remedies on a burn.
- Do not burst blisters.
- Do not remove clothing that is stuck to the burn.
- Do not wrap cling film tightly around a limb.

## When to get help
- Burns larger than the person's palm, deep burns (white, leathery or charred), or burns that do not hurt.
- Burns on the face, hands, feet, genitals, joints, or all the way around a limb.
- Electrical, chemical, or inhalation burns, and burns in children, the elderly or pregnant women.

## Red flags
- Burns to the face or airway, singed nostrils, hoarse voice, or difficulty breathing.
