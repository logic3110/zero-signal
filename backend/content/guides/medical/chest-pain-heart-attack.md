## Steps
1. Call the emergency number immediately.
2. Help the person sit in a comfortable position, usually half-sitting with knees bent and back supported.
3. Loosen tight clothing and keep them calm and still.
4. If they have their own prescribed angina medicine (for example a spray under the tongue), help them take it as prescribed.
5. If they are not allergic to aspirin and have no bleeding problem, emergency services may advise them to chew an aspirin; follow the dispatcher's or medicine label's instructions.
6. Stay with them and monitor breathing and responsiveness.
7. If they become unresponsive and stop breathing normally, start CPR and send for an AED.

## Warnings
- Do not let the person walk around or exert themselves.
- Do not give aspirin to someone who is allergic to it, under 16, or has been told not to take it.
- Do not wait to see if the pain goes away before calling for help.

## When to get help
- Any chest pain lasting more than a few minutes, or with sweating, nausea, breathlessness or pain spreading to the arm, jaw, neck or back.
- Women, older adults and people with diabetes may have milder or unusual symptoms such as breathlessness, tiredness or indigestion-like pain.

## Red flags
- Crushing or squeezing chest pain, grey sweaty skin, breathlessness, collapse.
