## Steps
1. For any animal bite or scratch, wash the wound immediately with soap and plenty of running water for 15 minutes.
2. Apply an antiseptic such as povidone-iodine if available.
3. Control bleeding with pressure and cover loosely; do not stitch or tightly close the wound.
4. Go to a health facility the same day for rabies vaccination and, if needed, rabies immunoglobulin, especially for bites from dogs, monkeys, cats or bats.
5. Note the animal's appearance and behaviour; if it is a pet, find out its vaccination status.
6. For a scorpion sting, keep the person still, apply a cold pack to the sting site, and go to a health facility. Children are at higher risk.

## Warnings
- Do not wait to see whether the animal gets sick before seeking rabies treatment.
- Do not apply chilli, turmeric, oil or other substances to the wound.
- Do not cut or suck a scorpion sting.

## When to get help
- All bites that break the skin from mammals need assessment for rabies and tetanus.
- Scorpion stings with sweating, vomiting, restlessness, breathing difficulty or chest pain need urgent care.

## Red flags
- Rabies, once symptoms appear, is almost always fatal; vaccination after a bite prevents it.
