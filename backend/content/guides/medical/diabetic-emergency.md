## Steps
1. Suspect low blood sugar in a person with diabetes who is shaky, sweaty, pale, hungry, irritable, confused or behaving oddly.
2. If they are awake and able to swallow safely, sit them down and give sugar: glucose tablets, a sugary (non-diet) drink, fruit juice, sweets or a spoon of sugar or honey.
3. Wait about 15 minutes. If they are not better, give sugar again.
4. Once they improve, give a snack with longer-lasting carbohydrate such as bread, biscuits or a meal.
5. If they have a glucose meter, help them check their blood sugar.
6. If they become drowsy or unresponsive, place them in the recovery position and call the emergency number.

## Warnings
- Do not give anything by mouth to someone who is unconscious or cannot swallow.
- Do not give insulin; it will make low blood sugar worse.
- If unsure whether sugar is high or low, giving sugar is safer.

## When to get help
- They do not improve after two doses of sugar, become unresponsive, or have a seizure.
- Very high sugar symptoms: great thirst, frequent urination, fruity breath, deep fast breathing, vomiting.
