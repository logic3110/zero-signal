## Steps
1. Keep the person still and support the injured part in the position you find it.
2. If bone is through the skin (open fracture), cover the wound with a clean dressing and control bleeding by pressing around, not on, the bone.
3. Check circulation beyond the injury: skin colour, warmth, feeling, and pulse at the wrist or foot.
4. Splint only if you must move the person or help is far away. Use something rigid (stick, rolled magazine, board) padded with cloth.
5. Immobilise the joint above and the joint below the break. Tie the splint with wide bands, not over the injury itself.
6. For an arm, use a sling to support the forearm across the chest. For a leg, you can tie it to the uninjured leg with padding between them.
7. Re-check circulation after splinting and every 15 minutes. Loosen ties if fingers or toes become cold, blue, numb or tingling.
8. Apply a cold pack wrapped in cloth for up to 20 minutes to reduce swelling, and watch for shock.

## Warnings
- Do not try to straighten a deformed limb unless there is no pulse beyond it and medical help is many hours away.
- Do not push a protruding bone back in.
- Do not let the person eat or drink if they may need surgery.

## When to get help
- All suspected fractures need medical assessment and an X-ray.
- Urgently if the limb is cold, pale or numb, the bone is through the skin, or the thigh or pelvis is injured.

## Red flags
- Suspected thigh or pelvis fracture (can cause major internal bleeding).
- No pulse, cold or blue skin beyond the injury.
