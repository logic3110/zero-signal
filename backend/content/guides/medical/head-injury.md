## Steps
1. If the person is unresponsive, check breathing and call the emergency number. Assume a neck injury too.
2. If they are awake, keep them still and sitting or lying down.
3. Control bleeding from the scalp with firm pressure using a clean cloth.
4. Apply a cold pack wrapped in cloth to a bump for up to 20 minutes.
5. Ask simple questions (name, place, what happened) to check how alert they are.
6. Watch them closely for at least 24 hours and do not leave them alone. Wake them gently every few hours the first night to check they respond normally.
7. Rest from physical activity, screens and alcohol until symptoms settle.

## Warnings
- Do not press hard on a scalp wound if you feel a dent or soft area in the skull; press around the edges.
- Do not give alcohol or sedating medicines.
- Do not let them drive or return to sport the same day.

## When to get help
- They were knocked out even briefly, have a seizure, or cannot remember the event.
- Repeated vomiting, worsening headache, confusion, drowsiness, slurred speech, weakness, or unequal pupils.
- Clear fluid or blood from the nose or ears, or bruising around the eyes or behind the ears.
- The person is elderly, on blood thinners, or a young child.

## Red flags
- Becoming more drowsy or confused, repeated vomiting, seizure, or unequal pupils.
