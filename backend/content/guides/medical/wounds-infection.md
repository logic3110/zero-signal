## Steps
1. Wash your hands (or wear gloves) before touching the wound.
2. Stop minor bleeding with gentle direct pressure for a few minutes.
3. Rinse the wound well with plenty of clean running or drinkable water for several minutes to wash out dirt.
4. Remove visible dirt with clean tweezers if it comes out easily.
5. Cover with a clean, non-stick dressing or plaster.
6. Change the dressing daily or when it gets wet or dirty, and re-check the wound.
7. Keep the wound clean and dry and watch for infection over the next days.

## Warnings
- Do not close a dirty or bite wound tightly; it can trap infection.
- Do not use cotton wool directly on a wound; fibres stick to it.
- Do not pour strong chemicals (e.g. undiluted bleach or kerosene) on wounds.

## When to get help
- The wound is deep, gaping, longer than about 2 cm, on the face, or over a joint.
- The wound came from an animal or human bite, rusty metal or soil, and tetanus vaccination is not up to date.
- Signs of infection: spreading redness, swelling, warmth, pus, red streaks or fever.

## Red flags
- Red streaks spreading from the wound, fever, or feeling very unwell.
