## Steps
1. Suspect anaphylaxis after exposure to an allergen (food, sting, medicine) with breathing difficulty, throat or tongue swelling, wheeze, faintness or collapse, often with hives.
2. Help the person use their adrenaline (epinephrine) auto-injector immediately: into the outer middle thigh, through clothing if needed, following the device instructions.
3. Call the emergency number and say "anaphylaxis".
4. Lay them flat and raise their legs. If breathing is difficult, let them sit up. If pregnant, lay them on their left side.
5. Remove the trigger if possible (for example scrape out a bee sting).
6. If there is no improvement after 5 minutes and a second auto-injector is available, give it.
7. Keep them lying down even if they feel better; symptoms can return.
8. If they become unresponsive and stop breathing normally, start CPR.

## Warnings
- Do not delay adrenaline to wait for symptoms to worsen.
- Do not let the person stand up or walk suddenly; they can collapse.
- Antihistamine tablets alone are not enough for anaphylaxis.

## When to get help
- Everyone who has had anaphylaxis or used an auto-injector must go to hospital, even if they recover.

## Red flags
- Difficulty breathing, swollen tongue or throat, hoarse voice, pale floppy child, collapse.
