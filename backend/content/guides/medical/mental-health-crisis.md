## Steps
1. Approach calmly, introduce yourself and find a quieter, safer place if possible.
2. Listen without judging. Let them talk and acknowledge their feelings.
3. For a panic attack, reassure them it will pass. Encourage slow breathing: breathe in for 4 counts, hold briefly, breathe out for 6 counts.
4. Ask directly and calmly if they are thinking about suicide. Asking does not put the idea in their head.
5. If they are at risk, do not leave them alone. Remove access to means of harm if safe to do so.
6. Help them contact someone they trust and professional help.
7. After a disaster, help with basic needs (water, food, shelter, contacting family) and link them with support.

## Warnings
- Do not argue, dismiss their feelings, or promise to keep suicidal thoughts secret.
- Do not put yourself in danger if the person is violent.
- Breathing into a paper bag is not recommended.

## When to get help
- Call the emergency number if someone has harmed themselves, has a plan to end their life, or is a danger to others.
- Chest pain or breathlessness should not be assumed to be panic; check for medical causes.
