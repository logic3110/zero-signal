## Steps
1. Move the person out of wind, rain and snow into shelter. Handle them gently and keep them lying down if they are very cold or drowsy.
2. Replace wet clothing with dry clothing, or cut it off to avoid excess movement.
3. Insulate them from the ground and wrap them in blankets, a sleeping bag or a survival bag, covering the head and neck. Add a vapour barrier (plastic) if available.
4. Warm the core: put warm (not hot) packs or bottles wrapped in cloth on the chest, armpits and back.
5. If fully alert and able to swallow, give warm sweet drinks and high-energy food.
6. For frostbite (white, hard, numb skin), protect the area and do not walk on frostbitten feet if avoidable.
7. Rewarm frostbitten parts only if there is no risk of refreezing: soak in warm water (comfortably warm to your own hand) for 15-30 minutes, then dress loosely with padding between fingers or toes.

## Warnings
- Do not rub frostbitten skin or massage cold limbs.
- Do not use direct high heat such as fires, heaters or hot water bottles against the skin.
- Do not give alcohol.
- Do not thaw frostbite if it may freeze again.

## When to get help
- Drowsiness, confusion, slurred speech, stopping shivering while still cold, or unconsciousness means severe hypothermia: call the emergency number.
- All frostbite needs medical assessment.

## Red flags
- Stopped shivering, confused or unresponsive, very slow breathing.
