## Steps
1. Call the emergency number or a midwife. Note the time contractions started and when waters broke.
2. Wash your hands well and prepare clean towels, a warm blanket, and a clean area.
3. Let the mother choose a comfortable position, such as half-sitting or on her side.
4. As the head appears, do not pull. Support it gently as it comes out. If the cord is around the neck, gently loosen it over the head.
5. The shoulders and body usually follow with the next contraction; support the baby and lift it onto the mother's chest, skin to skin.
6. Dry the baby immediately with a towel, cover both of them, and keep the baby warm. Wipe the mouth and nose.
7. If the baby does not breathe or cry within a minute of drying and rubbing the back, start newborn resuscitation with rescue breaths.
8. There is no need to cut the cord quickly. If help is far away, wait until it stops pulsing, then tie it tightly in two places and cut between them with a clean blade.
9. The placenta usually comes out within 30 minutes. Keep it for medics. Massage the lower belly firmly if bleeding is heavy, and encourage breastfeeding.

## Warnings
- Do not pull on the baby or the cord.
- Do not push the baby back in or ask the mother to cross her legs.
- Do not put anything into the vagina.

## When to get help
- Heavy bleeding after birth, a baby that does not breathe, the cord or a limb coming out first, fits, or a premature birth are emergencies.
- Mother and baby should both be checked by a health worker after any unplanned birth.

## Red flags
- Heavy bleeding, fits, severe headache or blurred vision in pregnancy, cord coming out first.
