## Steps
1. Check for danger, then check for a response: speak loudly and tap a child's shoulder or the sole of an infant's foot.
2. Shout for help. Open the airway: for a child tilt the head back and lift the chin; for an infant keep the head in a neutral (level) position.
3. Look for normal breathing for no more than 10 seconds.
4. If not breathing normally, give 5 rescue breaths: for a child seal your mouth over their mouth and pinch the nose; for an infant cover both mouth and nose with your mouth. Each breath should make the chest rise.
5. Give 30 chest compressions. Child: one or two hands in the centre of the chest, pushing about one third of the chest depth (about 5 cm). Infant: two fingers or two thumbs just below the nipple line, about 4 cm deep.
6. Keep a rate of 100-120 compressions per minute, then give 2 breaths. Continue 30 compressions to 2 breaths.
7. If you are alone with no phone, do CPR for about 1 minute before leaving to call for help; carry an infant with you if you can.
8. Use an AED as soon as one is available; use child pads if present, otherwise adult pads placed front and back.
9. Continue until the child breathes normally or help takes over.

## Warnings
- Do not tilt an infant's head far back; this can close the airway.
- Do not blow hard into an infant; use small puffs just enough to make the chest rise.
- Do not delay starting CPR to look for a pulse.

## When to get help
- Call the emergency number immediately; use a speaker phone so you can keep going.
- Any child who needed rescue breaths or CPR must be checked in hospital.

## Red flags
- Child or baby is limp, blue or grey and not breathing normally.
