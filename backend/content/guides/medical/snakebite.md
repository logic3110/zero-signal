## Steps
1. Move away from the snake. Do not try to catch or kill it; a photo from a safe distance may help.
2. Keep the person calm and as still as possible; movement spreads venom.
3. Remove rings, watches, bangles, anklets and tight clothing from the bitten limb before swelling starts.
4. Immobilise the bitten limb with a splint or sling, as you would for a fracture, and keep it at about heart level.
5. Note the time of the bite and any symptoms as they develop.
6. Transport the person to a hospital that has antivenom as quickly as possible, carrying them if you can. Call the emergency number.
7. If they stop breathing normally, give rescue breaths or CPR; some venoms cause paralysis of breathing.

## Warnings
- Do not cut the wound, suck out venom, or apply ice, heat, chemicals or herbs.
- Do not apply a tight tourniquet.
- Do not wait for symptoms before going to hospital; some bites (such as krait) cause few early symptoms but are deadly.
- Do not give alcohol or painkillers like aspirin.

## When to get help
- Every snakebite should be treated as an emergency and assessed in hospital.

## Red flags
- Drooping eyelids, difficulty swallowing or breathing, bleeding from gums or wound, severe swelling, collapse.
