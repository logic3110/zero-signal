## Steps
1. Protect yourself: wear gloves or use a plastic bag over your hand if available.
2. Call the emergency number or get someone else to call.
3. Find the source of bleeding and press directly on it firmly with a cloth, dressing or your hand. Keep pressing hard and do not lift to check.
4. For a deep wound, pack it: push gauze or clean cloth into the wound until full, then keep pressing on top for at least 3 minutes (10 minutes if not using haemostatic gauze).
5. If bleeding from an arm or leg does not stop with pressure, apply a tourniquet 5-7 cm above the wound, not over a joint. If the wound location is unclear, place it high on the limb.
6. Tighten the tourniquet (turn the windlass) until the bleeding stops, then lock it. It will hurt; this is expected.
7. Write the time the tourniquet was applied on the tourniquet or the person's forehead.
8. Keep the person lying down and warm, and watch for signs of shock.

## Warnings
- Do not loosen or remove a tourniquet once applied; leave that to medical staff.
- Do not use narrow cord, wire or thin string as a tourniquet; use a band at least 3-4 cm wide.
- Do not remove objects stuck deep in a wound; pad around them and press around the edges.
- Do not use a tourniquet on the neck, chest or abdomen; use packing and pressure there.

## When to get help
- Any bleeding that spurts, soaks through dressings, or needs a tourniquet needs emergency care.
- Seek care for wounds that are deep, gaping, from a bite, or contaminated.

## Red flags
- Blood spurting or pooling quickly.
- Pale, cold, sweaty, confused or drowsy person (shock).
