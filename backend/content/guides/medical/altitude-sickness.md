## Steps
1. Suspect acute mountain sickness (AMS) with headache plus nausea, poor appetite, tiredness, dizziness or poor sleep after going above about 2,500 m.
2. With mild AMS, stop going higher. Rest at the same altitude, drink enough fluids and avoid alcohol until symptoms go away.
3. If symptoms do not improve after a day of rest, or get worse, descend at least 300-1,000 m.
4. Check for HACE (brain swelling): confusion, unusual behaviour, drowsiness, or clumsiness. Test by asking them to walk heel-to-toe in a straight line.
5. Check for HAPE (fluid in lungs): breathlessness at rest, cough, gurgling breathing, pink frothy sputum, reduced ability to exercise.
6. For HACE or HAPE, descend immediately, even at night, with a companion. Keep the person warm and sitting upright if breathless.
7. Use supplemental oxygen or a portable pressure bag if available and you are trained, but do not let it delay descent.

## Warnings
- Do not go higher with symptoms of altitude sickness.
- Never leave a person with altitude sickness to descend alone.
- Do not assume it is just a cold, tiredness or dehydration.

## When to get help
- Any sign of HACE or HAPE, or symptoms that do not improve with rest or descent: seek emergency evacuation.

## Red flags
- Confusion, stumbling, breathlessness at rest, or pink frothy cough at altitude.
