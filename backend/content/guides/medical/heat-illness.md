## Steps
1. Move the person to shade or a cool place and remove excess clothing.
2. Check how alert they are. Confusion, strange behaviour, collapse or seizure means heatstroke: call the emergency number.
3. For heatstroke, cool immediately: immerse the body up to the neck in cool or cold water if possible. Otherwise soak them with water and fan constantly, and put cold packs or ice wrapped in cloth on the neck, armpits and groin.
4. Keep cooling until they are alert or help arrives.
5. For heat exhaustion (heavy sweating, weakness, headache, nausea, dizziness but thinking clearly), lie them down in the cool and raise the legs.
6. If fully alert and able to swallow, give sips of cool water or oral rehydration solution.
7. Watch them closely; heat exhaustion can progress to heatstroke.

## Warnings
- Do not give drinks to someone who is confused or drowsy.
- Do not give paracetamol or aspirin to lower heatstroke temperature; it does not work.
- Do not delay cooling while waiting for transport.

## When to get help
- Any confusion, collapse, seizure, or a temperature that stays high: this is heatstroke and needs emergency care.
- Heat exhaustion that does not improve within 30 minutes of rest and fluids.

## Red flags
- Hot skin with confusion, slurred speech, collapse or seizure.
