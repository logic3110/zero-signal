## Steps
1. Move away from the area calmly to avoid more stings.
2. Watch for signs of a severe allergic reaction: difficulty breathing, wheeze, swelling of the face, lips, tongue or throat, faintness or collapse. If any appear, follow the anaphylaxis guide and call the emergency number.
3. If a bee sting is still in the skin, scrape it out sideways with a fingernail or the edge of a card as soon as possible.
4. Wash the area with soap and water.
5. Apply a cold pack or a cloth soaked in cold water for 10 to 20 minutes to reduce pain and swelling.
6. Raise the stung arm or leg if it is swelling, and remove rings or watches near the sting before the swelling increases.
7. Over the next few days, watch for signs of infection: increasing redness, warmth, pus or fever.

## Warnings
- Do not squeeze the sting with fingers or tweezers; this can push more venom in.
- Do not scratch the sting; it increases swelling and the risk of infection.
- Do not wait to see whether breathing problems get worse; treat them as anaphylaxis straight away.

## When to get help
- Call the emergency number for any breathing difficulty, throat or tongue swelling, faintness or collapse.
- Get urgent help for stings in the mouth or throat, many stings at once, or a sting in a young child.
- See a health worker if swelling keeps spreading after 2 days or there are signs of infection.
- Anyone who has had a severe reaction to a sting before should seek care after any new sting, even if they feel well.

## Red flags
- Difficulty breathing, swollen tongue or throat, hoarse voice, dizziness or collapse after a sting.
