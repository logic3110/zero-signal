## What first aid is
- First aid is the immediate care you give someone who is ill or injured until professional help arrives or they recover.
- Its aims are to keep the person alive, stop their condition getting worse and help them recover.
- You do not need equipment or training to start: calling for help, keeping someone safe and pressing on a bleeding wound are all first aid.

## Steps
1. Check for danger to yourself, bystanders and the person, such as traffic, fire, electricity, water or gas. Do not approach until it is safe.
2. Check for a response: speak loudly and tap their shoulders.
3. Shout for help and call the emergency number, or ask someone else to call. Use speaker phone so your hands are free.
4. Open the airway: tilt the head back and lift the chin.
5. Check breathing for up to 10 seconds. If they are not breathing normally, start CPR (see the CPR guides).
6. If they are unresponsive but breathing normally, put them in the recovery position.
7. Look for and control severe bleeding with firm, direct pressure on the wound.
8. Then check for other injuries and illness, keep the person warm, calm and still, and stay with them until help arrives.
9. Note what happened and any changes, and tell the emergency services.

## Warnings
- Do not put yourself in danger; you cannot help anyone if you become a casualty.
- Do not move an injured person unless they are in immediate danger, especially after a fall or road accident.
- Do not give an unresponsive or drowsy person anything to eat or drink.
- Do not give medicines that are not the person's own unless the emergency service tells you to.

## When to get help
- Call the emergency number for anyone who is unresponsive, not breathing normally, bleeding heavily, has chest pain, difficulty breathing, a seizure, signs of stroke or a serious injury.
- If you are unsure how serious it is, call; the call taker can guide you.

## Red flags
- Not breathing normally, unresponsive, severe bleeding, choking, blue lips, or a rapidly worsening condition.
