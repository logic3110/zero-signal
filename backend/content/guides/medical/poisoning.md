## Steps
1. Make the area safe; move the person to fresh air if there are fumes (only if safe for you).
2. Check response and breathing. If unresponsive and not breathing normally, start CPR (use a barrier or compressions only if chemicals are on the mouth).
3. If unresponsive but breathing, place them in the recovery position.
4. Find out what was taken, how much and when. Keep the container, label or plant sample to show medics.
5. For chemicals on the skin, remove contaminated clothing and rinse the skin with plenty of water for 15-20 minutes. For chemicals in the eye, see the eye injury guide.
6. If they are awake and the substance burned the mouth, they may take small sips of water only if advised by a doctor or poison centre.
7. Call the emergency number or a poison information centre.

## Warnings
- Do not make the person vomit.
- Do not give salt water, milk, oil or home remedies.
- Do not give anything by mouth to a drowsy person.
- Pesticide on clothing or skin can poison you too; wear gloves.

## When to get help
- All poisonings need medical advice. Call the emergency number immediately if the person is drowsy, fitting, breathing abnormally, or swallowed a corrosive, kerosene, pesticide or medicines.

## Red flags
- Drowsiness, seizures, difficulty breathing, burns around the mouth, pinpoint pupils with drooling and sweating.
