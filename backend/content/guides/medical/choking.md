## Steps
1. Ask "Are you choking?" If they can cough strongly, encourage them to keep coughing and stay with them.
2. If they cannot cough, speak or breathe, stand to the side and slightly behind. Support the chest and lean them forward.
3. Give up to 5 firm back blows between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand, checking after each one.
4. If still choking, give up to 5 abdominal thrusts: stand behind, make a fist just above the navel, grasp it with your other hand and pull sharply inwards and upwards.
5. Alternate 5 back blows and 5 abdominal thrusts until the object comes out or the person becomes unresponsive.
6. For a pregnant woman or a very large person, use chest thrusts (hands on the lower breastbone) instead of abdominal thrusts.
7. For an infant under 1 year: lay the baby face down along your forearm with the head lower than the body, give up to 5 back blows, then turn face up and give up to 5 chest thrusts with two fingers in the centre of the chest.
8. If the person becomes unresponsive, lower them to the ground, call the emergency number and start CPR. Look in the mouth before rescue breaths and remove any object you can see.

## Warnings
- Do not do blind finger sweeps in the mouth.
- Do not give abdominal thrusts to infants under 1 year.
- Do not slap the back of someone who is coughing effectively.

## When to get help
- Anyone who received abdominal or chest thrusts should be checked by a doctor because of the risk of internal injury.
- Call the emergency number if the obstruction does not clear quickly.

## Red flags
- Cannot speak, cough or breathe; lips turning blue; clutching the throat.
