## Steps
1. Check the scene is safe for you, then tap the person's shoulders and shout to see if they respond.
2. Open the airway by tilting the head back and lifting the chin, then look for normal breathing for no more than 10 seconds. Gasping is not normal breathing.
3. If they are not breathing normally, call the emergency number (put the phone on speaker) and send someone to find an AED (defibrillator).
4. Kneel beside the chest. Put the heel of one hand in the centre of the chest (lower half of the breastbone) and the other hand on top.
5. Push straight down hard and fast: about 5-6 cm deep, 100-120 compressions per minute, letting the chest come fully back up after each push.
6. If you are trained and willing, give 2 rescue breaths after every 30 compressions. If not, keep doing compressions only without stopping.
7. As soon as an AED arrives, switch it on and follow its voice prompts. Keep compressions going while the pads are applied.
8. Continue until the person starts breathing normally, trained help takes over, or you are too exhausted to continue. Swap rescuers every 2 minutes if possible.
9. If they start breathing normally, place them in the recovery position and keep checking their breathing.

## Warnings
- Do not stop compressions for more than 10 seconds except when the AED tells you to.
- Do not worry about breaking ribs; effective compressions matter more.
- For a visibly pregnant woman, if a second person is available, have them push the belly gently to the person's left while you do compressions.
- Do not put anything in the person's mouth.

## When to get help
- Always call the emergency number as soon as you find someone not breathing normally.
- Anyone who has been resuscitated must be taken to hospital even if they seem to recover.

## Red flags
- No response and not breathing or only gasping.
- Collapse after chest pain or during exercise.
