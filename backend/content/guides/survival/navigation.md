## Steps
1. The sun rises roughly in the east and sets roughly in the west. At midday it is due south in the northern hemisphere and due north in the southern hemisphere.
2. Shadow stick: put a stick upright, mark the tip of its shadow, wait 15 minutes and mark again. A line from the first to the second mark points roughly east.
3. Watch method (northern hemisphere): point the hour hand at the sun. Halfway between the hour hand and 12 o'clock is south. Use 1 o'clock instead of 12 during daylight saving time.
4. At night in the northern hemisphere, find Polaris (North Star) using the two pointer stars of the Big Dipper; it is almost exactly north.
5. In the southern hemisphere, use the Southern Cross: extend its long axis about 4.5 times its length to find a point roughly above south.
6. Once you know direction, pick a distant landmark on your bearing, walk to it, then pick the next one.
7. Use the ZeroSignal compass to mark and follow a bearing if your phone has a magnetometer.

## Warnings
- Moss and tree growth are unreliable direction indicators.
- Do not move in fog or darkness unless you are in danger where you are.
