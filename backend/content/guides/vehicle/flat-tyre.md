## Steps
1. Pull off the road onto flat, firm ground as far from traffic as possible. Switch on hazard lights.
2. Apply the handbrake, put the car in first gear or Park, and get all passengers out to a safe place away from traffic.
3. Place a warning triangle 50-100 metres behind the car (further on fast roads) and wedge a stone or chock against the wheel diagonally opposite the flat.
4. Get out the spare, jack and wheel brace. Remove the wheel cover if fitted.
5. Loosen each wheel nut half a turn anticlockwise while the tyre is still on the ground.
6. Place the jack at the vehicle's jack point closest to the flat (see the owner's manual or the notch/mark on the sill) and raise until the tyre is about 5 cm off the ground.
7. Remove the nuts and the wheel. Fit the spare, then hand-tighten the nuts.
8. Lower the car until the tyre touches the ground, then tighten the nuts firmly in a star (criss-cross) pattern. Lower fully and tighten again.
9. Check the spare's pressure as soon as possible and have the wheel-nut tightness checked after 50 km.

## Warnings
- Never put any part of your body under a car supported only by a jack.
- Do not jack on soft ground, slopes or at points other than the jack points.
- Space-saver spares are temporary: keep below the speed marked on them (often 80 km/h).

## When to get help
- Call roadside assistance if you are on a motorway hard shoulder, a bend, in heavy traffic, or the nuts will not come loose.
