## Steps
1. If the pedal goes to the floor, pump it quickly several times to build pressure.
2. Take your foot off the accelerator and switch on hazard lights. Sound the horn to warn others.
3. Shift down one gear at a time (manual) or select a low gear (automatic) to use engine braking.
4. Apply the handbrake gradually and firmly; do not yank it at high speed.
5. Steer towards a safe run-off area: uphill road, gravel, bushes or an escape lane. Scrape against a barrier only as a last resort.
6. Once stopped, do not drive further. Check for leaking brake fluid near the wheels.
7. For soft or spongy brakes, grinding or squealing, or pulling to one side, drive slowly to a mechanic and have them checked.

## Warnings
- Do not switch the engine off while moving; you will lose power steering and brake assist.
- Do not keep driving with a brake warning light or a soft pedal.
- On long descents, use a low gear instead of riding the brakes to prevent brake fade.

## When to get help
- Any loss of braking, a brake warning light, or brake fluid leak needs a mechanic before driving on.
