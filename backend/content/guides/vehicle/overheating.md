## Steps
1. Switch off the air conditioning and turn the heater and fan to maximum hot; this draws heat away from the engine.
2. Pull over safely as soon as possible and switch off the engine.
3. Open the bonnet only if there is no steam coming out, and let the engine cool for at least 30 minutes.
4. When cool, check the coolant level in the see-through expansion tank against the MIN/MAX marks.
5. Look for leaks, a split hose, a broken fan belt, or a cooling fan that is not working.
6. Top up with coolant, or clean water in an emergency, only when the engine is cool.
7. Restart and watch the gauge closely. If it rises again, stop and get help.
8. On long hill climbs, use a lower gear and take breaks to avoid overheating.

## Warnings
- Never open the radiator or coolant cap while the engine is hot; boiling coolant can cause severe burns.
- Do not pour cold water onto a hot engine.
- Do not keep driving with the gauge in the red; the engine can be seriously damaged.

## When to get help
- The engine overheats again after topping up, there is a large leak, white smoke from the exhaust, or oil in the coolant.
